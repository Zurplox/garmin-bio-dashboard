Circadian Rhythm is your body's 24-hour internal clock — it controls when you feel sleepy, when you're alert, when your hormones peak, and when your body temperature rises/falls. Disrupting it (late nights, jet lag) degrades sleep quality and performance.

Ultradian Cycles are the 90-minute loops your brain cycles through during sleep: deep sleep → light sleep → REM → repeat. More complete cycles = more growth hormone released, more memory consolidated, more physical repair done.

4+ cycles (6+ hours) is the elite athlete minimum for tissue regeneration. 5 cycles (7.5h) is optimal.

The Melatonin Gate is your body's natural "sleep onset window" — the 30-minute period when your brain is primed to fall asleep. Missing this window makes it harder to fall asleep and reduces sleep quality.

Blood oxygen (SpO₂) is the share of your red blood cells carrying oxygen. At sea level most healthy adults sit between 95% and 100%; a sustained reading below 90% is the point at which a doctor should look at it.

This card therefore reports how many days actually carried a reading, and the latest one it took, instead of an average that would look nightly when it is not.

        How to read this chart:

        Each dot is one night's sleep. Side to side is your resting heart rate (RHR) — your pulse while you are lying still; a lower number normally means a stronger, more efficient heart. Up and down is overnight heart rate variability (HRV) — how much the gap between heartbeats varies; a higher number normally means your nervous system has recovered. The two dashed lines are your own 30-day averages, so the chart reads against you, not against strangers.

        They are read together because they show different things: RHR tells you how hard your heart is working at rest, HRV tells you how much recovery your nervous system got. The good combination is a low RHR with a high HRV. When both move the wrong way at once — HRV down and RHR up — that is the pattern that tends to show up before illness, a stretch of heavy training, or broken sleep.

        The goal is to have as many dots in the top-left quadrant (Q1) as possible.

        🟢 Q1: PEAK RECOVERY
        High HRV + Low RHR. Your nervous system is fully recovered. Train hard — your body is primed for peak performance.
        🟡 Q2: CARDIO AROUSAL
        High HRV + High RHR. Often after intense training or heat. HRV is good but heart rate is elevated — train moderately.
        🟣 Q3: PARASYMP FATIGUE
        Low HRV + Low RHR. Nervous system suppressed. Common with deep fatigue or illness onset. Prioritize sleep and rest.
        🔴 Q4: SYSTEMIC STRAIN
        Low HRV + High RHR. Worst recovery state. Indicates overtraining, illness, or high psychological stress. Rest is mandatory.
